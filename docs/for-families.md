# For families

Reader: a parent, a teacher, or someone who was hurt, looking for help now. No technical knowledge needed.

Last reviewed: Batch 2, 5 October 2026

This page is a first draft. It covers steps you can take today. The full guide comes in Batch 7, which will list facts country by country. We do not guess. What is on this page has been checked.

## If someone is in danger right now

Call your local emergency number.

## If a fake or real nude image exists

These steps are safe in any case:

- Do not share the image. Do not forward it. Do not copy it. This is true even if you send it to a friend to ask for advice.
- Do not post the image anywhere to ask for help.
- If the person in the image is under 18, report it to your national hotline or the police. Do not delay.

## Services that can help take images down

We have checked that these two services exist and read what they do. We have not yet checked how well they work in your country, or if they accept images made by a computer program.

- **Take It Down**. This is run by a US charity called NCMEC. It is for images of people who were under 18 when the image was made. The address is takeitdown.ncmec.org. The person can stay anonymous. The service works with a short code made from the image, so the image itself never leaves your device.
- **StopNCII.org**. This is a similar service for images of adults.

Please read each service's own site before you use it. Official pages are the safest source.

## Lower the risk

You can take steps to make an attack harder, without needing any new technology:

- **Limit who sees photos.** Check the privacy settings on social media. A private account is harder to scrape than a public one.
- **Talk to children.** Tell them not to share private images of themselves or their friends. Make sure they know they can come to you if something goes wrong.
- **If someone threatens you.** If a person says they will share a fake image unless you pay them or do what they say, do not pay. Stop talking to them. Report it to the platform and the police.

## The law in the United States

The United States has a law called the TAKE IT DOWN Act. It sets rules for how websites must remove these images. The FTC gives guidance for businesses on how to follow this law.

## What Mantelet cannot do for you

Mantelet does not protect anyone yet. There is no tool. Even if we build one, it cannot remove an image that is already online. Please read the [Limits](limits.md) page so you know exactly what this project cannot do.
