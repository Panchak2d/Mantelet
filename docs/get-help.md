# Get help if a fake nude image was made of you

This guide is for anyone who has found a fake nude or sexual image of themselves, or of a child they care for. The image may be made by a computer program. The harm is still real, and so is the help.

Mantelet does not protect anyone yet. This guide does not depend on our research. It uses services that exist today. No single service covers everything, so this guide shows several routes. Use more than one.

You did nothing wrong. The person who made or shared the image is responsible.

## Do these first

1. **If you or someone else is in danger now, call your local emergency number.**
2. **Do not pay anyone who threatens to share the image.** Paying rarely stops it. See [Blackmail](#if-someone-is-blackmailing-you).
3. **Do not share the image.** Do not forward it, even to a friend who wants to help.
4. **Pick your path below.** The steps differ by age.

| If this is you | Go to |
|---|---|
| You are 18 or older | [Path A](#path-a-you-are-18-or-older) |
| You are under 18, or the image was made when you were under 18 | [Path B](#path-b-you-are-under-18-or-the-image-shows-someone-under-18) |
| You are a parent or carer | [Path C](#path-c-parents-and-carers) |
| You work in a school | [Path D](#path-d-schools) |

## Path A: you are 18 or older

### Step 1: Write down where the image is

Make a private note. For each page, record the web address, the site name, the account name and the date you found it. Australia's eSafety Commissioner asks for the web address and the site name when you report.

You may also keep screenshots if you feel able to. The Cyber Civil Rights Initiative warns that removing images before evidence is collected can destroy evidence that police or lawyers need. Keep any screenshots private. Do not post them.

Search your own name, and try a reverse image search, to find more copies.

### Step 2: Use StopNCII.org

StopNCII.org is run by a UK charity, SWGfL. It is for adults. The image must show you and you must have it on your device.

- It accepts fake images. Its FAQ says synthetic images count if they show you and are nude or semi-nude.
- Your image never leaves your device. Your device makes a code, called a hash. Only the hash is sent. Taking part platforms use it to find and block matching images.
- You get a case number and a PIN. Nobody can recover them. Keep them safe. Without them you cannot check the case or withdraw it.

What it cannot do:

- It works only with the platforms that take part. It does not clear the whole internet.
- A cropped, filtered or clipped copy may need its own hash.
- You can add up to 20 images at one time. You cannot add to a case later. Start a new case.
- It does not work on encrypted messaging.

### Step 3: Report to each site and app

Report on every site that shows the image. Use the right form below. Do not give anyone a link to a tool that makes these images.

| Where | What to do |
|---|---|
| Google Search | Open the image in Google Images, choose More, then Remove result, then "It shows a sexual image of me". You can say it is a fake made with AI. You can add more images to one request. You can ask Google to filter future duplicates. You get an email when Google decides. |
| Bing and Microsoft | Use Microsoft's reporting form at aka.ms/reportci. It is for people 18 and over. Microsoft also works with StopNCII on Bing. |
| Facebook and Instagram | Use the in-app report. Meta says its teams review reports every day, in more than 70 languages. Meta takes part in StopNCII. |
| TikTok | Use the TikTok webform for sexual deepfakes, which TikTok calls "sexually explicit digital identity theft". TikTok says it sends updates by text or email. |
| Snapchat | Report the message, story or account in the app. Snap has report categories for intimate images. Snap takes part in StopNCII. |
| X | X bans intimate images made without consent, including a face put onto another person's nude body. Report the post in the app, or use the private information report form. |
| Discord | Use discord.com/report. For US residents, Discord says it will act within 48 hours under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. Its page says AI-made images count. |
| YouTube | Report under the Community Guidelines, use the privacy complaint form, or, in the US, use the TAKE IT DOWN Act form. |

Reports on other sites: look for a report or abuse link on the page. Many sites list a contact address in their terms.

### Step 4: Tell the police

Reporting to the police is your choice. It can help if the image was shared, if you know who made it, or if someone is blackmailing you. See [The law where you live](#the-law-where-you-live).

### Step 5: Get support

Helplines are in [Support for you](#support-for-you).

## Path B: you are under 18, or the image shows someone under 18

Tell an adult you trust. It can be a parent, a teacher, a school counsellor or a doctor.

### Do not copy, forward or store the image

This rule is different from the adult rule. Do not save, copy, forward, print or store the image. Do not take screenshots of it. England's guidance for schools says staff must never copy, print, share, store or save such images, and that this is illegal.

Instead, write down in text: the web address, the site name, the account name and the date. A trusted adult can keep this note.

### Step 1: Report to the police or a hotline

NCMEC says that using AI to make sexual or nude images of a child should always be reported and taken seriously.

- **United States:** NCMEC CyberTipline at missingkids.org/cybertipline. Reports go to law enforcement. For help, call or text 1-800-THE-LOST (1-800-843-5678).
- **Canada:** Cybertip.ca.
- **United Kingdom:** Report Remove (below), and the police.
- **Australia:** eSafety Commissioner, and the police through ReportCyber. Call 000 if a child is in danger now.
- **India:** Childline 1098 is a national, free, 24-hour helpline for children. You can also use the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal or call 1930.
- **Europe and other countries:** INHOPE links 58 hotlines in 53 countries. Find yours at inhope.org.

### Step 2: Use Take It Down

Take It Down is run by NCMEC, a US charity. It is for images taken when you were under 18, even if you are an adult now.

- The image stays on your device. Only a hash is sent.
- You do not have to give personal information.
- It works in more than 30 languages.
- NCMEC's generative AI page says Take It Down can help with a sexual image "whether real or GAI-created". The tool's own FAQ does not mention AI. So also report to the police or a hotline, and to each platform.

What it cannot do: it does not stop uploads to other sites. It does not work on encrypted platforms.

### Step 3: In the UK, use Report Remove

Report Remove is a free tool from Childline and the Internet Watch Foundation. It is for people under 18 in the UK. Childline's page says it includes fakes made using AI. Childline never sees your pictures, videos or links. Only the IWF does. It cannot remove images from encrypted networks such as WhatsApp, or from a phone or computer.

### Step 4: Report to the platform

Use the forms in Path A, Step 3. Where a form asks your age, say it. Google sends reports about under-18s to its form for child sexual abuse material. Microsoft asks you to report under-18 images as child sexual exploitation and abuse imagery.

## Path C: parents and carers

- **Stay calm and listen.** Check how your child is. Ask if they want support. The eSafety Commissioner says to put wellbeing first.
- **Thank them for telling you.** Praise a child who got a fake and did not share it.
- **Do not view, copy, print, share or store the image.** Collect only text: links, usernames and dates.
- **Report** using [Path B](#path-b-you-are-under-18-or-the-image-shows-someone-under-18). Then talk to the school.
- **If your child made or shared a fake,** listen first. Explain that this can be a crime and that the harm is real. The eSafety Commissioner suggests encouraging them to delete the content, apologise and report it so platforms can remove copies. If an adult or a threat is involved, call the police.

## Path D: schools

- **Put wellbeing first.** Support the student or staff member who was targeted.
- **Tell your designated safeguarding lead or equivalent.** England's guidance says to refer to the police or children's social care at once if an adult is involved, a child is at immediate risk, or there is blackmail.
- **Do not view, copy, print, share or store the image.** Keep a written record: what happened, where, links, account names and dates.
- **Help the student report.** In the UK, they can use Report Remove. Elsewhere use [Path B](#path-b-you-are-under-18-or-the-image-shows-someone-under-18).
- **Know the support.** A US survey found that only 5 percent of teachers said their school gave victims resources to get fake images removed. Plan before an incident.
- Canada's Centre for Child Protection has a free guide for schools. It covers real, edited and AI-made images.

## If someone is blackmailing you

Blackmail with a nude image is called sextortion. The image may be real or fake.

- **Do not pay.** NCMEC says to get help before you pay or comply with the blackmailer.
- **Block the person, but do not delete your own profile or messages.** NCMEC says that can help stop the blackmailer.
- **Report the account on the platform.**
- **Report to the police.** In the US, tell the police or the FBI. In Australia, if you are being blackmailed, report to eSafety under illegal and restricted content. Elsewhere, call your local police.
- **If you are under 18,** use [Path B](#path-b-you-are-under-18-or-the-image-shows-someone-under-18). NCMEC: the blackmailer is to blame, not you.

## What these tools cannot do

- **No tool removes an image from the whole internet.** Hash tools work only on the platforms that take part.
- **Encrypted messaging is out of reach.** Hash tools and Report Remove do not work there.
- **Copies can come back.** An edited copy may not match a hash.
- **Not every site obeys.** Some sites ignore reports. Use the legal routes below.
- **Speed varies.** Some sites state a time limit. Others do not.

## The law where you live

Laws change. This table says what we found, and its limits. It is not legal advice. Ask a lawyer or a helpline.

| Place | What we found |
|---|---|
| United States | The TAKE IT DOWN Act makes covered platforms offer a way to ask for removal. They must remove the image, and known identical copies, within 48 hours of a valid request. The FTC says it covers real images, altered images and AI deepfakes. If a platform fails, report it at TakeItDown.ftc.gov. The FTC now enforces the law. A federal right to sue, the DEFIANCE Act, has passed the Senate. At last check it had not passed the House. State laws differ. We did not check each state. |
| United Kingdom | Sharing, or threatening to share, an intimate image without consent is an offence, including altered images. Creating or asking for a fake intimate image of an adult is an offence in England and Wales (Sexual Offences Act 2003, sections 66E and 66F, in force since 6 February 2026). Scotland and Northern Ireland have different laws. Computer-made sexual images of children fall under indecent images laws. |
| Canada | Bill C-16 changed the Criminal Code. Its provisions took effect on 18 July 2026. The intimate-image offence now covers computer-made sexual images of an identifiable person that could be mistaken for a real recording. A threat to share one is also an offence. Law-firm summaries put the maximum at 10 years. We did not read the statute itself. Creating an image alone may not be an offence. |
| Australia | Sharing sexual material without consent is a federal offence, whether or not it was altered or made with technology. The maximum is 6 years in prison, or 7 for the aggravated offences. eSafety can ask a site to remove intimate images. eSafety has said its complaint schemes cover synthetic material. |
| India | The government's rules for platforms say intermediaries must remove flagged non-consensual intimate images, including "morphed" images, within 24 hours of a complaint. You can complain through the platform's grievance officer, the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal or 1930, a One Stop Centre, or the police. The IT Act 2000 and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 have offences that may apply. For children, the POCSO Act applies. We did not check section numbers. Sources disagree on shorter 2026 deadlines. |
| European Union | The EU's 2024 directive on violence against women requires member states to make non-consensual sharing of intimate images a crime. It took effect in June 2024. States must put it into national law by June 2027. Until then, national law decides. The Digital Services Act lets you flag illegal content on platforms, get reasons for their decisions and appeal. |
